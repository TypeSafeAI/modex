app, not the engine on its own. After a clean quit it replays each thread's journal against its saved

## 8 MB slow-stream fallback

`test/question-replay.test.ts` runs this with a real host over TLS:

1. A real paired desktop's credentials open `/events` with a journal cursor, then stop reading.
2. A question is answered.
3. 512 KB items stream past.

**What happens:**
- The host's write backlog for that stream grew by about 0.5 MB per item. A probe logged 7.87 MB just before the drop.
- Once it passed 8 MB, the host dropped the stream.
- The client's reconnect from its old cursor would have to replay more than 4 MB, so the host answers `resumed: false` and replays nothing.
- The client's reload (`thread:items`) shows the card answered.
- Its stale answer is refused, and Codex heard one answer.

A client that has stopped reading never reads the end of its stream either, so the test checks the drop on the host.

The host can also drop a stalled stream another way. Every 15 s its heartbeat tries a write, and a stream that
can't take it is ended, even below 8 MB. So a stalled stream is dropped within 15 s, or as soon as it passes 8 MB,
whichever comes first. A test that only waits for a reconnect can't tell the two apart. Raising the 8 MB limit to
1 GB still let such a test pass, through the heartbeat. [event-journal.md](../event-journal.md) now names both paths.

## Power loss (proxy)

These tests cover a dying process:
- **A question answered from the phone, process killed inside the batch window** (`test/question-replay.test.ts`). The answer is saved and handed to Codex, and its journal rows are still in memory when the process dies. The next process opens the journal, finds it was not closed cleanly, and takes a new id. The phone's old cursor reloads, and nothing is sent again.
- **An approval answered, then a real SIGKILL** (`test/journal-resume.test.ts`).
- **An Electron process SIGKILLed** (`e2e/event-journal.spec.ts`).

- **The app SIGKILLed in the middle of a streaming reply** (`e2e/event-journal.spec.ts`). The test streams a mock
  reply of 400 words, one every 30 ms, and kills the app 120 ms into a batch. The relaunch takes a new journal id.
  The thread is idle with one user message, and the reply reads `unknown` at its saved length. Passed on
  2026-10-22: at the kill, the journal held 326 characters of the reply and the thread file 305. So the journal can
  be *ahead* of the state files at a crash, not only behind. Each saves on its own 250 ms timer, and the state
  files win. [event-journal.md](../event-journal.md) now says so.

**Not tested:** losing power with `synchronous = NORMAL` can roll back rows that were already sent to clients. A cursor past those rows would then be accepted and could skip later events. Simulating that needs a power cut or a VM snapshot revert. It stays open, as in the [CF-6 review](https://github.com/TypeSafeAI/modex/issues/279).

## Physical iPhone: reconnect after sleep and a network change

**Needs:**
- a Mac running a Modex build from this branch or later, with `MODEX_EVENT_JOURNAL=1`;
- a Companion build cut after #373, so it draws question cards (TestFlight is Val's);
- a Codex account.

**Steps:**
1. Pair the iPhone from **iPhone companion** on the Mac by scanning its code.
2. On the Mac, start a Codex thread with **Plan mode** on and ask: "Add a greeting.txt for new users; the language isn't decided." Wait until the question card shows on both the Mac and the phone.
3. On the phone, pick an option but don't send. Lock the phone for 30 s.
4. While the phone is locked, answer the question on the Mac.
5. Unlock the phone and open the thread.
   - **Pass:** within about 3 s the card reads "Answered on your Mac", its controls and your choice are gone, and the plan appears as the answer.
   - **Fail:** the card is still answerable, or a second answer reaches Codex (check the Mac's transcript).
6. Ask for another plan. When the question shows, switch the phone from Wi-Fi to cellular and back to Wi-Fi (or between two Wi-Fi networks). Answer on the phone once it says connected.
   - **Pass:** the Mac's card reads "Answered on your phone", and Codex gets one answer.
   - **Fail:** an error, a stuck card, or two answers.
7. Repeat step 6, but press Stop on the Mac while the phone is off the network.
   - **Pass:** on reconnect, the card reads "Not answered — the turn was stopped", and nothing can be sent.

## Windows and Linux

**On CI.** The `Native platform builds` workflow runs the journal, replay and request suites on `windows-latest`
and `ubuntu-latest`: `event-journal`, `journal-resume`, `request-journal`, `request-crash`,
`conversation-migration`, `question-replay` and `desktop-item-kinds`. Each run uploads a TAP file as the
`native-journal-<platform>` artifact.
- **Before this rebase,** without `question-replay`: Linux passed 50 of 50
  ([run 39887710207](https://github.com/TypeSafeAI/modex/actions/runs/39887710207)). Windows passed 49 of 50. The
  failure was a test assumption: on Windows a self-SIGKILL ends the process with an exit code, so `spawnSync`
  reports no signal. With the assertion fixed, both passed 50 of 50
  ([run 39890594811](https://github.com/TypeSafeAI/modex/actions/runs/39890594811)).
- **The run on this PR,** with `question-replay`, is the record for the final head.

**The app** (deferred by Val; the steps stay here in case it is run):

Needs: a Windows 11 and a Linux desktop (Ubuntu 24.04 with GNOME or KDE), each with the installer from
this branch's build and `MODEX_EVENT_JOURNAL=1`.

**Steps:**
1. On each machine, run `pnpm install && pnpm build && pnpm test` from a checkout.
   - **Pass:** the journal, replay and question suites pass: `event-journal`, `journal-resume`, `question-replay`, `request-journal`, `conversation-migration`.
2. Launch Modex there with `MODEX_EVENT_JOURNAL=1`. Start a Codex thread with Plan mode on, and answer its question.
   - **Pass:** the card reads answered, and Codex gets one answer.
3. Start another Plan turn. Kill Modex (Task Manager's End task, or `kill -9`) right after answering its question, then relaunch.
   - **Pass:** the card reads answered or "Delivery uncertain", never answerable, and nothing runs again.

Paired desktops are the Store app, paired from a Mac host's **Desktop access → Pair a desktop** menu. They're covered on macOS by the tests above.
